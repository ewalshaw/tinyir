The Northern Lights

The northern lights, or aurora borealis, appear as colorful curtains of light in polar skies. Charged particles from the sun collide with gases in Earth's atmosphere. Oxygen can produce green and red glows, while nitrogen may create blue or purple tones.

Clear, dark nights far from city lights offer the best viewing conditions. Photographers use long exposures to capture the shifting patterns. Auroras remind us that Earth is constantly interacting with space weather.
