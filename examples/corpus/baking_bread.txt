How Bread Rises

Bread rises because yeast ferments sugar and releases carbon dioxide gas. The gas gets trapped in a network of gluten, making the dough expand. Gluten forms when flour mixes with water and is kneaded.

Warm temperatures help yeast work faster, but too much heat can kill it. Salt controls fermentation and strengthens flavor. Bakers watch proofing time carefully to get a light, airy loaf with a crisp crust.
